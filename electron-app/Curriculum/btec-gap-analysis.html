Pearson BTEC - Robotics Specification Audit

Robot Arm - BTEC Robotics Specification Gap Analysis

A line-by-line audit of the 16-module robot arm curriculum against the four Pearson BTEC qualifications that explicitly specify robotics as their subject matter. Where the companion Learning Objectives Mapping asks “which module delivers this theme?”, this document asks the harder question: “for every item of published unit content, can a learner actually evidence it on this platform?”

    Level 3 · Internal · 60 GLH · Specification Issue 10, June 2026 · Learning aims A–D, content A1–D2
  • Unit 4068 Industrial Robots - Pearson BTEC Higher Nationals in Engineering (unit code L/617/3940)
    Level 4 · 15 credits · Unit Descriptors Issue 6, October 2025 · LO1–LO4
  • Unit 4015 Automation, Robotics and Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) - Pearson BTEC Higher Nationals in Engineering (unit code M/651/0731)

    Level 3 · 55 GLH total · Specification Issue 2, May 2023 · Robotics Systems & Basic Procedures; Fieldbus & Reference Systems; Motion Programming; Further Robot Programming

Why these four. The companion mapping document correctly notes that the general BTEC Engineering specifications never name “robotics”. These four do - they are the only Pearson units whose published content is robot-specific rather than robot-adjacent, so they are the only ones against which a genuine coverage claim can be tested. Unit 56 and the HN units are the realistic delivery targets; the Specialist Award is included because it is the most granular published statement of what a robot-operations learner is expected to do, even though its content is written around a specific vendor controller (Comau C5G / PDL2 / WinC5G).

Coverage scorecard

Each row of the tables below is one item - a bullet of published unit content, or a tight group of related bullets. Percentages are indicative rather than exact, and a raw item count does not weight items by how many assessment criteria hang off them (the PLC gap in Unit 4015 is far heavier than its row count suggests). The shape of each bar is the finding: the curriculum is strong where the arm itself is the teaching object, and weak wherever the specification assumes an industrial cell around it.

The one-paragraph verdict

The curriculum is a strong and near-complete vehicle for the robot half of these specifications - kinematics, coordinate frames, closed-loop servo control, motion profiles, safety standards, cell design, diagnostics and project skills are all genuinely taught with hands-on activities, and in several places (PID depth, live telemetry, software architecture) they exceed what the specs ask for. The gaps cluster into four blind spots, all of which share a cause: the curriculum teaches the arm, whereas the specifications describe an industrial installation. Those blind spots are maintenance, discrete sensors and industrial I/O, PLCs and fieldbus, and the commercial/Industry 4.0 context. Three of the four are fixable with new written modules and no hardware change.

Spec 01

Unit 56: Industrial Robotics

L3

BTEC Level 3 National, 60 GLH, internally assessed across a maximum of three assignments: A (A.P1, A.P2, A.M1, A.D1) · B and C combined (B.P3, B.P4, C.P5, C.P6, B.M2, C.M3, BC.D2) · D (D.P7, D.P8, D.M4, D.D3). This is the single most important target: it is the unit a UK centre would actually register learners on.

A1 Health and safety requirements

Defining industrial robotics

Standalone robots; manufacturing work cells / robot installations

 Covered

MOD 06 distinguishes robot types and applications; MOD 12 defines the work cell explicitly and has learners design one.

Relevant regulations

Health & safety at work legislation; Electricity at Work Regulations / PAT; HSE publications; MTA Safeguarding Codes of Practice - Industrial Robots parts 1–3

 Partial

MOD 13 covers HASAWA 1974, PUWER 1998, ISO 10218-1/-2 and ISO/TS 15066 well. Missing: the Electricity at Work Regulations and portable appliance testing, HSE guidance publications, and the MTA Safeguarding Codes of Practice, which the spec names explicitly.

Human dangers

During programming, during maintenance, and as a result of system faults

 Partial

MOD 01 covers pinch/crush and unexpected motion; MOD 13 covers kinetic energy and speed. The spec's three-phase framing is not used: danger during programming (the highest-risk phase in industry, because the operator is inside the envelope) and during maintenance are not treated as distinct hazard categories.

Safety systems and their effectiveness

Dead man's handle; hold and emergency stop buttons; pressure pads / matting; laser light guards; electromagnetic field barriers

 Partial

Light curtains, interlocks, guarding, e-stop and pressure matting all appear in MOD 13. Missing: the dead man's handle / three-position enabling device - a core industrial concept and one the arm's torque-enable control is a natural analogue for - plus hold buttons and EMF barriers. The spec also wants effectiveness evaluated, not just listed.
A2 Maintenance

Inspection routines

Mechanical condition of all parts; environmental conditions - particulate matter, temperature, ventilation, shock, vibration, electrical noise

 Gap

No module teaches an inspection routine. MOD 10 reads temperature and load telemetry, which is adjacent but is framed as diagnostics, not scheduled preventive inspection.

Spare parts to sustain continuous operation

Seals, bearings, motors, pneumatic and hydraulic lines and connectors, end effector parts

 Gap

Not covered anywhere. The ST3215 servos, bus cabling, and end-tool parts are a perfectly good basis for a consumables/spares discussion.

Maintenance tools and test equipment

Safe isolation kit, grease gun, allen keys, spanners, socket sets, multimeter, mastering gauges, tool and base reference points

 Gap

Not covered. Mastering gauges and tool/base reference points are especially notable - the arm does have a calibration jig (the ESP32S3 calibration hardware in this repository), which is exactly a mastering fixture, but the curriculum never introduces it.

Set-up, maintenance procedures, and their overall effectiveness

Standard operating procedures; evaluating whether the maintenance regime works

 Gap

No maintenance procedure is written or performed. This is the most consequential single gap in the document: A2 underpins A.P2, A.M1 and A.D1, so one of the unit's three assignments cannot currently be evidenced at any grade.
B1 Principles of operation and their applications

Operational characteristics

Automated, repeatable, tireless, replaces human effort

 Covered

MOD 06 levels of automation; repeatability quantified in MOD 12 cycle-time work.

Specifications

Number of axes, payload, size and shape of working envelope

 Covered

MOD 02 range of motion, MOD 05 payload vs reach, MOD 12 envelope and dead zones.

Types of controller, manipulator, end effector / tooling

Pneumatic suction cup; hydraulic; electrical and mechanical grippers

 Partial

Electrical/mechanical grippers are well covered. Pneumatic and hydraulic tooling receive no coverage at all - no mention of suction cups, vacuum generators or hydraulic actuation anywhere in the 16 modules.

Workspace organisation

Feed of work, robot-to-robot work, material flow and logistics

 Partial

MOD 12 covers part presentation, operator access and layout. Missing: robot-to-robot working and material flow / logistics through a line.

Applications of robots and their impact

Manufacturing, inspection, palletising, working alongside humans, replacing human workers

 Partial

MOD 06 covers most application types and cobots. Palletising is never named (a staple industrial application), and impact - the displacement-of-workers question - is raised only as an optional extension question.
B2 Design principles

Types of robotic system with associated working envelope

Cylindrical, spherical, jointed, Cartesian, SCARA

 Partial

MOD 06 teaches Articulated, SCARA, Delta and Cobot. Three of the five named configurations are absent: cylindrical, spherical, and Cartesian/gantry. (Delta and cobot are good additions but are not substitutes - the spec names the five it wants.) Working envelope shape per configuration is also not drawn.

Reference frames

World, joint, tool

 Covered

MOD 07 is strong here - right-hand rule, base/world frame, tool frame, and reading orientation out of the homogeneous transformation matrix.

Wrist articulations

Yaw, pitch and roll; degrees of freedom as translations and rotations

 Gap

Degrees of freedom are covered, but the terms yaw and pitch never appear in the curriculum and wrist articulation is not analysed as a roll–pitch–roll (or yaw/pitch/roll) chain. This is core L3 vocabulary and the arm has the wrist axes to demonstrate it.

Drive mechanisms

Mechanical (ball screws, chain/belt, gears), pneumatic, hydraulic, electrical

 Partial

Gears and gear reduction are covered well in MOD 02, and electrical drive is thoroughly covered. Missing: ball screws, chain and belt drives, and all pneumatic/hydraulic actuation. B.M2 explicitly asks learners to compare drive systems across two robots, which is not currently possible.
B3 Control systems

Closed-loop servo controlled systems

e.g. for driving one axis of a robot

 Covered

Exceeds the requirement. MOD 03 covers open- vs closed-loop and magnetic position sensing; MOD 08 goes into PID in depth, well beyond L3 expectations.

Input, output and feedback signals

The sequence that takes place to perform a task

 Covered

MOD 03 live status data, MOD 10 telemetry, MOD 11 full-system signal trace.
C1 Sensors

Sensor types

Tactile (microswitch / piezoelectric / strain gauge / pressure); non-tactile (capacitive / inductive / light / laser); vision (inspection, identification, navigation)

 Gap

The curriculum covers the servos' internal magnetic encoders and the ArUco camera, and nothing else. Not one of the named tactile or non-tactile sensor types appears anywhere - no microswitch, piezoelectric, strain gauge, pressure, capacitive, inductive, photoelectric or laser sensing. C.P5 requires the operating principles of three different sensor types, so this Pass criterion currently fails.

Sensor applications

Safety, work-cell control, component / part inspection

 Partial

The Camera tab and ArUco detection give a genuine identification application, referenced in MOD 06 and MOD 11. But vision is treated as an integration example, not taught as a sensor; and safety-rated and work-cell-control sensing are not covered.
C2 End effectors

Grippers and tools

Parts handling / transfer, assembly, welding, paint spraying, testing

 Partial

Handling and transfer are covered thoroughly through pick-and-place; welding and painting are named in MOD 06 as applications but their tooling is not described. C.P6 needs two end effector types explained by operating principle - currently only the servo gripper is.

Tool centre point

Tool calibration for different applications

 Partial

TCP appears in MOD 07 as a coordinate concept. Missing: TCP calibration as a procedure - measuring a tool, re-declaring the TCP when tooling changes, and verifying it. This is one of the highest-value additions available, because it is both examinable here and central to the Robotic Operations award.
D1 · D2 IDE, programming principles and implementation

Overview of the functions of an IDE

Simulator, pure code, available tools

 Partial

The app provides a genuine dual environment - Blockly (visual) and the G-code editor (pure code) - covered in MOD 04 and MOD 09. The missing piece is the simulator, and it matters: D.P8 is a Pass criterion that requires a program “tested in simulation mode”. The 3D view and the non-moving FK/IK tester are the raw material for this but are never framed or taught as a simulation mode.

Types of programming

Online, lead through, offline

 Partial

Online programming is covered thoroughly (pendant, jogging, teach-and-playback in MOD 12). Lead-through is absent despite the hardware supporting it perfectly - disabling torque lets a learner physically hand-guide the arm and record points, which is lead-through programming. Offline programming is absent.

Subroutines and subprograms

Used to streamline programming methods

 Gap

Program structure is never taught. No module introduces subroutines, reusable blocks or program decomposition, in either Blockly or G-code. The same gap appears in the Robotic Operations award (MAIN + ROUTINES) - fixing it once serves both.

Design of programs from a brief

Design specification, flowchart, structured English, safety considerations

 Gap

Learners write programs, but never design one first. Flowcharting appears once in passing and structured English (pseudocode) never appears at all. D.P7 is “Design an industrial robot program” - the design artefact is the evidence, and there is currently no activity that produces it.

Motion commands

Point to point, linear, circular

 Partial

Point-to-point (G0) and linear (G1) are taught excellently in MOD 09 and contrasted in MOD 12 including the path-quality and cycle-time trade-off. Circular / arc interpolation is the missing third - not supported by the G-code processor and not taught.

The importance of avoiding singularities

During programming

 Gap

The word “singularity” does not appear in any of the 16 modules. Note that the companion objectives mapping currently claims MOD 07 delivers “explain joint limits and singularities” - that claim is not supported by the module content and should be corrected or the content added. Named explicitly in both Unit 56 D2 and the L4 units.

Work-cell commands

Wait, signal, delay

 Partial

Delay/dwell is taught (G04) and used well. “Signal” and “wait-on-input” - handshaking with other cell equipment - are absent, which is the same underlying gap as the missing discrete I/O. The ESP32 end tool's digital and PWM outputs are an available hook for this.

Program selection, start-up, test, alterations and operation

Full operational lifecycle of a stored program

 Covered

Covered across MOD 04, MOD 09 and MOD 12 via stored positions, G-code files and run/stop control.

Testing

Simulator; code debugging; running live with no workpieces; incrementing operating speeds; decreasing operator input

 Partial

Speed ramping is done properly - MOD 12 has learners time cycles at 50% and 75%, which is exactly the spec's intent. Dry-running without workpieces and progressive reduction of operator input are not formalised, and systematic debugging is not taught as a method.
Spec 02

Unit 4068: Industrial Robots

L4

Higher National, 15 credits. The natural progression unit from Unit 56 and the closest HN match to this platform. LO1 and LO2 are well served; LO3 is served in substance but not in industrial tooling; LO4 is essentially unaddressed.

LO1Operational characteristics, selection criteria and applications

Configurations

Cartesian, cylindrical, spherical, toroidal, SCARA

 Partial

Same gap as Unit 56 B2, one configuration wider - toroidal is also named here. MOD 06 currently delivers SCARA plus articulated, delta and cobot.

Selection criteria - LOSTPED

Load, orientation, speed, travel, precision, environment, duty cycle; number of axes; anthropomorphic robots

 Gap

The LOSTPED framework is never introduced. This directly blocks P2 (“Describe selection criteria for industrial robot applications”) and feeds D1. Individually the curriculum teaches load (MOD 05), speed and precision (MOD 08) and travel/envelope (MOD 12) - it is the named framework that is missing, not the underlying physics, which makes this a cheap fix.

Common brands

Fanuc, Yaskawa, ABB

 Partial

ABB and Fanuc get passing mentions; Yaskawa none. No comparison of vendor ecosystems.

Applications

Welding, painting, material handling, packaging, assembly, inspection, hazardous environments, repetitive tasks

 Covered

MOD 06 covers the application landscape well, including the commercial reasoning behind robot selection.

Controller architecture

Motion controller, motor drives, power supplies, HMI

 Covered

A genuine strength. MOD 03 and MOD 09 map the real stack; MOD 11 traces a command through every layer. Deeper than most centres can offer, because the source is open.

Manipulator

Sensing, brakes, axis motor, effector motor, environment sensing

 Partial

Axis and effector motors and internal sensing are covered. Brakes are absent - including the safety-critical question of what holds a loaded arm up when power is removed, which the arm demonstrates negatively every time torque is disabled. Environment sensing is limited to the camera.

Axis operation

Purpose of each axis, work area, reach, wrist roll/pitch/yaw, rotation, home position and calibration

 Partial

Axis purpose, work area, reach and home position are covered. Wrist roll/pitch/yaw and axis calibration are not - the same gaps as Unit 56 B2 and C2.

End effectors

Two-jaw, vacuum and magnetic grippers; tooling interfaces

 Partial

Two-jaw is the platform's own gripper. Vacuum and magnetic are named only in passing; tooling interfaces (mechanical and electrical coupling, tool changers) are not covered.
LO2Safety standards associated with industrial robots

Functional safety

IEC 61508; hazard and risk assessment

 Partial

Hazard identification and risk assessment are taught well in MOD 01 and MOD 13. Functional safety as a discipline is missing - no IEC 61508, no SIL or performance-level concept. P3 asks for “the principles and methods of functional safety analysis”, which needs this vocabulary.

Robot system safety standards

ANSI/RIA R15.06-2012; BS EN ISO 10218:2011

 Partial

ISO 10218-1/-2 and ISO/TS 15066 are covered properly in MOD 13. ANSI/RIA R15.06 (the US counterpart) is not mentioned - a small addition.

Cell safety features

Operating envelope, space restrictions, safeguards, e-stops, guarding, barriers, interlocks, light curtains, laser scanners, two-hand controls, floor mats; barrier sizing (around, under, through, over)

 Partial

Most items are present in MOD 13, and software dead zones in MOD 12 are a strong practical treatment of envelope restriction. Missing: two-hand controls, area scanners, and barrier sizing / safety distance calculation - the quantitative part, and the part that distinguishes L4 from L3.

Operational modes and user interfaces

Auto / manual / teach mode and their differing safety rules

 Partial

The app has torque enable/disable and control-session locking, but the industrial mode model (automatic vs manual reduced-speed vs teach, and the different protections active in each) is not taught. Appears in three of the four specifications audited.

Safety-first culture

Policies, procedures, regulations, compliance, risk management and mitigation

 Covered

MOD 13 handles the hierarchy of controls and engineering responsibility thoughtfully, including the legal and ethical framing.
LO3Program an industrial robot for automated process application

Programming levels

Joint-level, robot-level and high-level programming

 Covered

Unusually well served. Joint Control is joint-level, G-code Cartesian moves are robot-level, and Blockly/Python are high-level - all three exist as real interfaces to the same machine, which few teaching platforms can offer.

Tasking and command-and-control methods

Drag-and-drop, scripted language, point-n-click GUIs, lead by the nose

 Partial

Drag-and-drop (Blockly), scripted (G-code, Python) and GUI methods all covered. “Lead by the nose” is missing though the hardware supports it - see the lead-through note under Unit 56 D2.

Online programming

Joysticks, pendants, jogging, modifying existing positions

 Covered

Pendant tab, jogging and stored-position editing across MOD 04 and MOD 12.

Computer simulation / offline programming

Programming without the physical robot, then deploying

 Gap

Not taught. As with Unit 56 D1, the 3D view is the obvious basis for a simulate-then-deploy workflow, and building that would close a criterion in three of the four audited specs at once.

Controlling robots with PLCs

Cross-referenced to the PLC unit

 Gap

Not covered - see Spec 03, where this is the dominant finding.

Robot commands

Motion, interlock and sensor commands

 Partial

Motion commands are strong. Interlock and sensor commands are absent - the arm cannot currently wait on an external input, which is the atom of all cell integration.

Manufacturers' languages and toolchains

ABB RAPID, KUKA KRL, Yaskawa Inform; RobotStudio, Roboguide, MotoSim, WinCaps

 Platform

Vendor toolchains cannot run against this arm. However, a comparison exercise is entirely deliverable - set the same pick-and-place task in G-code and show the RAPID and KRL equivalents side by side. P5 asks learners to investigate the range of languages, not to use them all.
LO4Global economic scope and integration into smart factories

Economic scope

Major markets (Japan, USA, China, South Korea, Germany); application demand by sector; robot density; impact on and training of the workforce

 Gap

Not covered. “Robot density” - the defining metric of this LO - never appears. The only trace is an optional extension question in MOD 06 pointing at IFR statistics. P7 and D3 both sit here. This is desk-based content requiring no hardware, making it among the cheapest gaps to close.

Advances in robot technology

Machine vision, AI, cobots, IoT, edge computing, networked and cloud robotics, VR, robot training, role in Industry 4.0

 Gap

Cobots are covered in MOD 06 and the camera gives a vision foothold. Everything else is absent - the phrase “Industry 4.0” does not appear once in the curriculum, despite being the framing concept for this LO, for Unit 4015's LO3, and for Unit 56's own introduction. M4 and P8 are unevidenced.
Spec 03

Unit 4015: Automation, Robotics and PLCs

L4

Higher National, 15 credits, and the specialist unit on the HNC in Automation and Control Engineering. This is the starkest result in the audit, and it is structural rather than incidental: two of the four learning outcomes - LO1 and LO2, carrying P1–P5, M1, M2, D1 and D2 - are about Programmable Logic Controllers, and the curriculum contains no PLC content whatsoever.

LO1Design and operational characteristics of a PLC system

PLC system types and characteristics

Modular, unitary and rack-mounted; speed, memory, scan time, voltage and current limits; internal architecture

 Gap

No coverage. The word “PLC” appears nowhere in the 16 modules. Blocks P1 and D1.

I/O devices and interface requirements

Digital and analogue input and output devices

 Gap

No coverage of discrete industrial I/O. Blocks P2. The ESP32 end-tool's digital and PWM outputs are the nearest available hardware and are documented in the repository's register map, but the curriculum does not use them this way.

Communication standards and industrial networks

RS-232/422/485, Ethernet; supervisory / cell / field-level networks; PROFINET, PROFIBUS, AS-Interface, EtherNet/IP, Powerlink; configuration and commissioning

 Partial

A near miss worth acting on. MOD 08 teaches a real half-duplex multi-drop serial bus - frame structure, addressing, checksums, baud rate, latency, timeouts - which is conceptually exactly a field-level industrial network, and teaches it with more rigour than most PLC courses manage. What is missing is only the naming and the hierarchy: the ST3215 bus is never positioned against RS-485, PROFIBUS or AS-Interface, so learners cannot transfer the understanding they already have.

Programming languages and the wider control estate

IEC 61131-3 languages; PLC, VSD, HMI and SCADA

 Gap

IEC 61131-3 (ladder, function block, structured text, instruction list, SFC) is absent. HMI is a genuine strength - MOD 04 covers HMI design principles properly - but VSDs and SCADA are not covered, and SCADA appears only once in passing.
LO2Design a PLC program

Programming fundamentals

Signal types; number systems (binary, octal, hex); I/O allocation lists; logic functions AND/OR/XOR; timers, counters, latches

 Gap

No coverage. Blocks P4. Note that Blockly already exposes logic and loop constructs, so the conceptual bridge from Blockly logic to ladder logic is short - a comparison module would carry real weight here.

Communication techniques and network methods

PLC, HMI and SCADA configuration and programming; modern context of PLC programming and automation

 Gap

No coverage. Blocks P5 and D2.

Test and debug methods

Systematic testing and debugging, appropriately applied

 Partial

Transferable content already exists. MOD 10 teaches symptom→cause diagnostic reasoning with live telemetry - a genuinely systematic debugging method. It is not framed as a general technique, and it is not applied to program logic, but the teaching is there to be generalised (M2).
LO3Program industrial robots - elements and functions

Element considerations

Types of robots; sensors, tools and end effectors; programming methods; key commands; configuration, calibration and fault finding

 Covered

Well served across MOD 06, MOD 09 and MOD 10, with fault finding a particular strength. Calibration is the weak element (see Unit 56 C2).

Mobile robotics

AGVs, AMRs and mobile manipulators

 Gap

Not covered. Purely theoretical content; no hardware implication.

Robot manipulators

Kinematics, design, dynamics and control; vision systems; user interfaces; instrumentation configuration and calibration

 Partial

Kinematics (MOD 07), design, control (MOD 08) and user interfaces are all strong. Dynamics is absent - MOD 05 stops at statics, so inertia, acceleration torque and payload dynamics are untreated. Inverse kinematics is described conceptually but never derived or worked.

Data collection tools and formats

Effective use of data collection systems; input/output data formats

 Covered

MOD 10 logging and telemetry, with structured recording tables.

Impact of Industry 4.0

Smart factories; data and digital technologies; performance optimisation; documentation control processes; integration and organisational impact

 Gap

As at Unit 4068 LO4 - Industry 4.0 is never named. Performance optimisation is partly served by cycle-time work in MOD 12, and documentation practice by MOD 15, but documentation control - version, access, authorisation, location - is not covered.
LO4Design and safe operation of a robot in an industrial application

Health and safety policies, hazards, risk assessment and mitigation

Policies, procedures and regulations

 Covered

The strongest area in the whole audit. MOD 01 risk assessment and MOD 13 hazard register and hierarchy of controls meet P8 and M4 comfortably.

Cell safety features and operating envelope

Guarding, restriction, safe working plan

 Covered

MOD 12 cell design with dead zones plus MOD 13 safety audit; D4 (“design a safe working plan … including a full risk assessment”) is achievable today.

Operational modes and user interfaces

Automatic, manual reduced-speed, teach

 Partial

Same gap as Unit 4068 LO2 - the industrial mode model is not taught, though the interfaces themselves are covered thoroughly.
Spec 04

BTEC International Specialist Award in Robotic Operations

L3

Four mandatory units, 55 GLH. Read this section as a source of specific, examinable procedures rather than as a delivery target: the content is written around a particular vendor controller, so some items (WinC5G, PDL2 keywords) are not portable. The underlying operations, however, are exactly what industry expects of a robot technician - and several of them are absent here while being cheap to add.

U1Robotics Systems and Basic Procedures

Main components of the arm

Motors, gearboxes, mechanical structure, basic wiring

 Covered

MOD 02 structure and gear reduction; MOD 03 wiring, power and servo internals.

Main components of the control unit

Operator panel, power modules, CPU, battery, connections panel, power supplies, safety boards, electromechanical parts

 Partial

The Pi controller, power system and connections are covered. Missing: dedicated safety boards/relays, backup battery for position retention, and the concept of a separate controller cabinet - the platform genuinely lacks these, so theory delivery is needed.

Teach pendant operating modes and layout

Program / local / remote modes; mode selector, emergency stop, enabling device, key colour conventions; JOG movement; GUI page structure

 Partial

Jogging and GUI navigation are covered well in MOD 04. Missing: the mode selector and the three-position enabling device - the two controls that define industrial pendant safety. This is the third specification in this audit to name operating modes.

Degrees of protection and IP ratings

Need for protection; IP0x to IP6x; minimum four ratings, including two IP6x cases

 Gap

Entirely absent - IP ratings appear nowhere in the curriculum. An entire learning outcome (U1 LO C) rests on this, and it is pure theory: a one-page table and three scenario questions would close it.

Control unit procedures

Power up, restart, shutdown; drive on/off; login; creating users with different privileges

 Gap

Startup and connection are covered in MOD 03, but there is no user-account or privilege model in the app or curriculum - the control-session lock is the closest analogue and is a different concept. Operator/programmer/maintainer privilege separation is standard on industrial controllers.
U2Fieldbus and Reference Systems

Fieldbus and distributed control systems

MODBUS, PROFIBUS, INTERBUS, CAN; PROFIBUS-PA, Foundation Fieldbus; BACnet, LonWorks

 Gap

No named fieldbus is covered. As noted at Unit 4015 LO1, MOD 08 already teaches the underlying principles thoroughly on the ST3215 bus - this is a labelling and context gap, not a conceptual one.

Distributed I/O types

Digital (single-bit Boolean), analogue (16-bit), and flexible (1–32 bit) inputs and outputs

 Gap

Not covered. The ESP32 end tool (bus ID 64) exposes exactly this kind of register-mapped digital and analogue I/O and is documented in the repository - available hardware that the curriculum does not yet exploit.

3D reference systems

Base/World and the right-hand rule; user frames / work objects; tool coordinate system and TCP

 Partial

World/base, right-hand rule and tool frame are taught properly in MOD 07. User frames / work objects are entirely absent - the idea that a program can be written relative to a fixture and then re-based when the fixture moves. This is the single most useful industrial concept missing from the kinematics teaching.

Tool setup calculations

Standard method (measuring a tool against a known tool); four-point method (jogging about the TCP through P1–P4)

 Gap

Not covered, and highly recommended. The four-point TCP calibration is the defining practical skill of robot setup, it is directly assessable, it needs no new hardware, and it simultaneously serves Unit 56 C2 and Unit 4068 LO1.

Payload declaration and calibration checks

Consequences of incorrect payload; base/tool/robot calibration and validation; automatic payload identification

 Partial

MOD 05 handles payload physics very well - load vs pose, torque limits, effect of reach. What is missing is payload as a declared parameter: telling the controller what it is carrying, and what goes wrong when that declaration is absent or wrong.
U3Motion Programming

JOINT vs LINEAR trajectories

Recognising and justifying the appropriate trajectory for a movement command

 Covered

Exemplary. MOD 09 teaches G0 (PTP) vs G1 (linear interpolated) with the real mechanism explained, and MOD 12 has learners measure the cycle-time and path-quality consequences of the choice. This meets A.2 and A.3 fully.

Creating, checking, editing and saving a program

Full program lifecycle, recording positions

 Covered

Stored positions, G-code files and Blockly programs across MOD 04, MOD 09, MOD 12.

Continuous / blended movement

Fly-by motion and the blend-percentage variable; termination types

 Gap

Not covered. Motion blending - passing near a waypoint without stopping - is how real cycle times are cut, and it is the natural next step after the existing speed/acceleration work in MOD 08. Requires controller support as well as teaching.

Circular trajectories

Creating and checking a circular move; orientation control through the arc

 Gap

Not covered; the G-code processor supports G0 and G1 but no arc command. Named in Unit 56 D2 as well, so this gap is double-counted against two specifications.

Structured programs

A MAIN program with subprograms; ROUTINES; collision detection

 Gap

Program structure is not taught in any form - the same gap as Unit 56 D2. Collision detection gets brief mention in the cell-design context but not as a programmable controller function.
U4Further Robot Programming

System memory and backup / restore

Execution, storage and external memory; program and configuration files; backup, restore and reload procedures; turn-set and calibration procedures

 Gap

Not covered. Backup and restore of programs and configuration is a real technician competence and is trivially demonstrable here - positions are persisted locally and the servo configuration is readable over the bus. Pairs naturally with the missing maintenance module.

Vendor software (WinC5G)

Cooperative motion, joint soft servo, sensor tracking, palletising, conveyor tracking

 Platform

Vendor-specific and not portable. The capabilities it lists (conveyor tracking, palletising motion, sensor tracking) are worth naming in the industrial-context module as things advanced controllers do.

Program efficiency

Identifying repeated operations; debugging with cause-and-remedy; incremental jog

 Partial

Cycle-time optimisation is done well in MOD 12 and diagnostic reasoning in MOD 10. Missing: refactoring repeated operations into reusable routines - which depends on the subroutine gap above.

Planning and implementing an automated solution

Automation opportunity analysis, robot and end-effector selection, timescale, safety requirements; programming, testing, backup, efficiency

 Covered

Well served. MOD 12 cell design, MOD 14 planning and Gantt, MOD 16 evaluation. Only the backup step is missing.
Action

Priority gaps, ranked

Ranked by assessment consequence first (does a Pass criterion currently fail?), then by how many of the four specifications the gap appears in, then by cost to close. The first six are written content and require no hardware change.

01Maintenance - an entire assignment cannot be evidenced

Unit 56 learning aim A is half safety and half maintenance, and the maintenance half is completely absent: inspection routines, environmental conditions, spare parts, maintenance tools and test equipment, and the procedures themselves. Nothing in the 16 modules addresses preventive maintenance.

Blocks: Unit 56 A.P2, A.M1, A.D1 - one of three assignments, at every grade

Fix: a new module - Maintenance & Servicing. Write a standard operating procedure for inspecting the arm; carry it out and record it on a standard form; identify the consumables and spares (servos, bus cabling, gripper parts); list the tools required. Fold in the existing ESP32S3 calibration jig as the mastering fixture - the spec explicitly names mastering gauges and tool/base reference points, and that hardware already exists in this project.

02Discrete sensors - a Pass criterion needs three types, the arm teaches none

C.P5 requires the operating principles of three different robotic sensor types. The curriculum covers the servos' internal encoders and the ArUco camera. No microswitch, piezoelectric, strain gauge, pressure, capacitive, inductive, photoelectric or laser sensing appears anywhere.

Blocks: Unit 56 C.P5, C.M3, BC.D2 · weakens Unit 4068 LO1, Unit 4015 LO3

Fix: teach sensor principles as theory with a small practical hook. Even without new hardware, the magnetic encoder is a real non-tactile sensor worth analysing properly, and the camera gives vision. Adding one inductive and one microswitch input to the ESP32 end tool would convert this from a theory gap into a full practical.

03Program design and structure - no design artefact, no subroutines

Learners write programs but never design them first. Flowcharting appears once; structured English never. Subroutines and program decomposition are untaught in both Blockly and G-code. D.P7 asks learners to design a program, and the design document is the evidence.

Blocks: Unit 56 D.P7, D.M4, D.D3 · Robotic Ops U3 LO C, U4 LO C

Fix: extend MOD 09 with a design-before-code stage - brief → flowchart → structured English → code → test. Introduce Blockly functions and G-code subprogram calls as the refactoring step. Cheap, and it lifts the whole programming strand.

04Simulation mode - required by a Pass criterion, and nearly built already

D.P8 requires a program “tested in simulation mode”. The app has a 3D view and a kinematics tester that deliberately does not move the arm - the raw material for a simulate-then-deploy workflow - but it is never framed or taught as one, and there is no dry-run mode for a whole program.

Blocks: Unit 56 D.P8, D1 · Unit 4068 LO3 (offline programming) · Unit 4015 LO2 (test methods)

Fix: the highest-leverage app change in this document. A “dry run” toggle that executes a G-code or Blockly program against the 3D view with the bus writes suppressed would close a criterion in three of the four specifications at once.

05Industry 4.0 and the commercial context - pure theory, entirely absent

The phrase “Industry 4.0” does not appear once in the curriculum, despite framing an entire HN learning outcome and appearing in Unit 56's own introduction. Robot density, major markets, workforce impact, AI, IoT, edge and cloud robotics are all unaddressed.

Blocks: Unit 4068 P7, P8, M4, D3 (all of LO4) · Unit 4015 LO3

Fix: a new desk-based module - Robotics in the Global Economy. IFR robot-density data, market and sector analysis, workforce displacement and reskilling, and the technology-advances survey. No hardware, no lab time, and it closes the largest single block of L4 criteria in the audit.

06TCP calibration, user frames and payload declaration

Three related setup skills that industry treats as the core of robot technician competence, and that the curriculum touches conceptually but never as procedures: four-point TCP calibration, user frames / work objects, and declaring payload to the controller.

Blocks: Unit 56 C2 · Unit 4068 LO1 · Robotic Ops U2 LO B, LO C

Fix: extend MOD 07 with a practical calibration session. Four-point TCP calibration needs only a fixed pointer and careful jogging. User frames follow naturally from the existing transformation-matrix teaching.

07PLCs and fieldbus - 50% of Unit 4015, and a labelling gap away from half-solved

Unit 4015's LO1 and LO2 are entirely PLC content and are entirely absent, taking P1–P5, M1, M2, D1 and D2 with them. No named fieldbus appears either.

Blocks: Unit 4015 LO1 and LO2 in full · Unit 4068 LO3 · Robotic Ops U2 LO A

Fix, in two parts. The cheap half first: MOD 08 already teaches a real multi-drop serial bus with framing, addressing, checksums and timing - position it explicitly against RS-485, PROFIBUS and AS-Interface and the fieldbus gap largely closes on existing content. The PLC half genuinely needs new material (and, for full delivery, a PLC trainer); a Blockly-logic-to-ladder-logic comparison module would bridge a useful part of it without hardware. Alternatively, treat Unit 4015 as out of scope and target Unit 4068 instead - it is the better fit for this platform by a wide margin.

08Smaller named items, cheap to close

Each of these is absent, appears by name in at least one specification, and needs a paragraph to a page:

Singularities (Unit 56 D2 - and note the companion objectives document currently claims this is covered in MOD 07; that claim should be corrected or the content added) · Wrist yaw/pitch/roll · Cylindrical, spherical, toroidal and Cartesian configurations · LOSTPED selection criteria · IP ratings (an entire Robotic Operations learning outcome) · Operational modes - auto / manual reduced-speed / teach (named in three of the four specs) · Dead man's handle / three-position enabling device · Lead-through programming (the hardware already supports it - torque off and hand-guide) · Pneumatic and hydraulic drives and tooling · Ball screw, chain and belt drives · Circular / arc interpolation · Motion blending · Program backup and restore · Robot brakes · Palletising · MTA Safeguarding Codes of Practice, IEC 61508, ANSI/RIA R15.06 · Barrier sizing and safety distance calculation · Mobile robotics · Manipulator dynamics (MOD 05 stops at statics).

Where the platform sets a genuine limit

A small number of items cannot be demonstrated on this hardware at any level of curriculum effort. These should be planned as theory delivery with secondary evidence (video, site visit, guest speaker, case study) - which all four specifications permit - rather than treated as curriculum failures:

  • Hydraulic and pneumatic robot drives - a syringe or bench rig demonstration, as Pearson's own delivery guide for Unit 56 suggests, is the recognised workaround.
  • Vendor toolchains and languages - RobotStudio, Roboguide, MotoSim, RAPID, KRL, Inform. A side-by-side code comparison satisfies “investigate the range” without a licence.
  • Physical guarding, light curtains, safety relays and area scanners - the arm has no cell hardware; software dead zones in MOD 12 are the available substitute and are used well.
  • IP66-rated equipment and real ingress testing - theory and scenario-based assessment only, which is exactly what the Robotic Operations spec asks for.
  • Industrial PLC hardware - closing Unit 4015 LO1/LO2 properly needs a PLC trainer alongside the arm.
  • Payload-scale forces and industrial cycle rates - the physics is faithfully taught in MOD 05; only the magnitudes differ.
Method. Each specification's published unit content was taken bullet by bullet and searched against the full text of all 16 curriculum modules - teaching text, activities, worksheets and quiz questions. A topic was marked Covered only where a learner could produce evidence from a module activity; Partial where the idea is present but below the depth the assessment criteria demand, or where the content is named without being taught; Gap where the term and concept are absent from all 16 modules. Item counts are at sub-topic granularity and are indicative - the intent is to show where effort is best spent, not to produce an audit figure.

Note on the companion document. The BTEC Learning Objectives Mapping maps themes to modules and remains accurate as a delivery overview. One claim in it is not supported by the module content - that MOD 07 delivers “joint limits and singularities” - and should be corrected or the content written.
